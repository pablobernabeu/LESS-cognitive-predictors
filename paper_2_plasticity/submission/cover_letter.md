# Cover letter (draft)

[Date]

Dear Editor,

We are pleased to submit our manuscript, "Cognitive and resting-state EEG predictors of
intensive multilingual learning, and change in executive function across training: A
Bayesian multilevel study", for consideration as a Research Article in Bilingualism:
Language and Cognition.

Main contribution and interest for the readership. The paper relates baseline working
memory, inhibitory control, statistical learning and resting-state EEG to the trial-level
trajectory of grammaticality-judgement accuracy as Norwegian–English bilinguals learned an
artificial language over six sessions, and tests pre/post change in executive function
across the same training. The learning trajectory is modelled jointly at trial level, so
the level a learner reaches and the rate at which it is reached are estimated without
the two-stage per-learner slopes on which most aptitude studies rest, and the paper
reports how reliably each quantity is estimated. The resting spectrum enters twice, as
raw band power and as its aperiodic-adjusted decomposition, so the choice between them
is tested on the data. Working memory was the one predictor associated with overall
accuracy, credibly so in the fully sampled property, and working-memory span itself rose
across training, whereas inhibitory control, statistical learning and the resting
measures showed no credible association. The question of which domain-general capacity
supports adult grammar learning, and whether it changes with that learning, sits at the
centre of the readership's interests, and the study's answer is a measured one that also
shows why slope-based aptitude findings disagree.

A companion manuscript reporting the electrophysiological and behavioural signatures of
transfer in the same sample is under consideration at the Journal of Neurolinguistics.
The two papers share participants and design and analyse different outcomes; the
companion is cited as a submitted manuscript and is uploaded as a file not for
publication.

The manuscript is not under consideration elsewhere, all authors have approved it, the
study was preregistered on the Open Science Framework, and the data, code and materials
are available as stated in the Data availability statement. The corresponding author's
ORCID is 0000-0003-1083-2460.
